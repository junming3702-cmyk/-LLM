## Scope guard examples

- An issued tender clause states that if a clarification is made, deadline effect `E` follows. Compare that conditional effect with admitted law `R`; lack of proof that a clarification **actually occurred** is not a decisive gap for this clause-design task.
- A Level-1 law and Level-2 implementation regulation address one duty. Test whether they operate together; different wording alone is neither a norm conflict nor a tender-clause violation.
- A located bid sentence says a named qualification proof was *not provided*, and an admitted applicable legal requirement requires it: this may be a specific risk candidate. A proof absent only from the short LLM excerpt is `current_input_not_transmitted`, not proof of package omission.
- A bid technical paragraph merely describes quality-control methods, or a BoQ line has arithmetic: retain an auxiliary/not-reviewed boundary, not legal N or legal risk. A technical-bid paragraph that states a specific statutory duty may be re-routed individually.
- A tender requirement and bid paragraph share a section number but no independently matching substance: pairing remains unresolved; do not call the bid nonresponsive.
- A local law found online for another province, or a 2024 standard asserted against an older event without a verified effective-date bridge, cannot independently establish this project's risk.
